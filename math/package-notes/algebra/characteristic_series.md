# `characteristic_series` notes

[Package API](../../../libs/algebra/characteristic_series/README.md) · [Checking](../../../libs/USAGE.md)

## Construction

Construct rational symmetric polynomials, finite-precision formal series, and Chern-character/Todd/L/A-hat multiplicative sequences.
Dependencies: `std` and `algebra`.

### Conventions

`Polynomials[n].SymmetricPolynomial` is the subtype of polynomials whose rational evaluations are invariant under root permutations.
Construct elementary symmetric functions recursively on root lists; prove invariance for permutations generated by adjacent swaps and list extension.
`Symmetric.Change.elementaryNatural` connects formal polynomials with evaluated elementary symmetric functions.

`Symmetric.Newton.PowerSums.powerSum k xs` is the ordinary degree-\(k\) power sum.
Newton identities prove the following with \(p_k=\sum_i x_i^k\) and \(e_0=1\):
\[
\sum_{i=0}^{n}(-1)^i e_{n-i}p_{i+1}=(n+1)e_{n+1}
\]
The proof inducts on root extension using coefficient lists of \(E(t)=\prod_i(1+x_i t)\) and convolution with \(\sum_i x_i/(1+x_i t)\).

### Precision and operations

`Series.cut n p` stores \(n\) coefficients in degrees \(0,\ldots,n-1\), padding missing coefficients with zero.
`extend n m` represents precision \(n+m\).
`cutPlus`, `cutScale`, `cutTimes` and `Laws.cutInverseUnit`, `Laws.cutCompose`, `FormalSeries.cutInverse`, `Exponential.cutExp` give compatibility with lower-precision computation.
`trimPlus`, `trimScale`, `trimTimes` and `sameInverse`, `sameCompose` prove results independent of irrelevant higher input coefficients.

`Series.inverseUnit n u` is the inverse of \(1+t u(t)\).
`FormalSeries.inverse n a u` is the inverse of \(a+t u(t)\); `a` is the nonzero-rational subtype.
Both multiply with the original series to give one.
`Series.compose n p u` and `Exponential.exp n u` truncate \(p(tu(t))\) and \(\exp(tu(t))\), respectively.

`Genus.product` and `Genus.sum` return elements of existing abstract polynomial rings.
`productRepresentation`, `sumRepresentation` give finite-coefficient representations; `finiteBridge`, `finiteSumBridge` identify these with truncated-series computations.
`multiplicative` is the product law for concatenated roots; `additive` is Chern-character additivity.
`Genus.Precision.cutCh`, `cutTodd`, `cutL`, `cutAhat` prove compatibility when changing both generating-series and result precision.

### Degree-four/eight computations

Assign Chern roots \(x_i\) cohomological degree two; set \(c_j=e_j(x_i)\), \(p_j=e_j(x_i^2)\).
`Characteristic.Tables` checks the following coefficient table through \(t^4\).

| Series | \(t^0\) | \(t^1\) | \(t^2\) | \(t^3\) | \(t^4\) |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| \(e^t\) | 1 | 1 | \(1/2\) | \(1/6\) | \(1/24\) |
| \(t/(1-e^{-t})\) | 1 | \(1/2\) | \(1/12\) | 0 | \(-1/720\) |
| \(t/\tanh t\) | 1 | 0 | \(1/3\) | 0 | \(-1/45\) |
| \((t/2)/\sinh(t/2)\) | 1 | 0 | \(-1/24\) | 0 | \(7/5760\) |

Generating series are constructed at arbitrary precision.
Todd is the unit inverse of \((1-e^{-t})/t\); L is \(\cosh t\) times the unit inverse of \(\sinh(t)/t\); A-hat is the unit inverse of \(\sinh(t/2)/(t/2)\).

`*Finite` in `Genus.DegreeEight` computes finite-polynomial coefficients by these universal formulas:
\[
\begin{aligned}
\operatorname{ch}&=r+c_1+\frac{c_1^2-2c_2}{2}
 +\frac{c_1^3-3c_1c_2+3c_3}{6}
 +\frac{c_1^4-4c_1^2c_2+2c_2^2+4c_1c_3-4c_4}{24},\\
\operatorname{Td}&=1+\frac{c_1}{2}+\frac{c_1^2+c_2}{12}
 +\frac{c_1c_2}{24}
 +\frac{-c_1^4+4c_1^2c_2+3c_2^2+c_1c_3-c_4}{720},\\
L&=1+\frac{p_1}{3}+\frac{7p_2-p_1^2}{45},\\
\widehat A&=1-\frac{p_1}{24}+\frac{7p_1^2-4p_2}{5760}.
\end{aligned}
\]
These equalities are truncated at degree eight.
Induct on root count, proving degreewise root-addition identities using commutative-ring laws and concrete rational computations.

### Proof generation

`tools/generate.py` generates equality proofs for coefficient tables and universal formulas.
Generated `.ref` is checked normally; Python results are not imported as axioms or decision rules.
Check rational computations through existing quotient APIs, common-denominator addition, and fraction expansion, avoiding direct reduction of large cross products.
Ring computations are explicit equality proofs using associativity, commutativity, distributivity, and inverse laws.

```sh
python3 libs/algebra/characteristic_series/tools/generate.py
```
